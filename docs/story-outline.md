# Club Night — Story Outline (DRAFT for review)

Sep 26, 2026 · drafted by Claude for Joseph to edit

Built from the Design Document and the Character Tone Guide. Everything here is a proposal. Marked **[?]** where a decision is yours.

## The story in one line

A struggling club, a newcomer who keeps getting better, and a rival who is lovely to everyone and somehow always one step ahead.

## What it's about underneath

- **Belonging.** You arrive knowing nobody. By the end, the club is yours, and you're one of the people keeping it going.
- **Fairness that isn't.** Favouritism dressed up as fairness: Coach Pemberton's choices, Toby's easy rise, Vera's old grievance. Nobody is a villain, which is the whole problem.
- **Improvement, felt quietly.** The story never says "you're getting better." The characters notice, in their own petty ways.

## The four acts at a glance

| 1 — Club nights | Trial night, then learning the ropes | You and Toby both join. He's instantly liked. The club is short of money and members. | Club knockout cup final: Toby |

| 3 — The league season | Match nights; the room-hire stakes | The pub raises the room hire; Graham's spreadsheets say the club needs a good season. Mid-season, Toby leaves for Kingsbridge ("Oh — I assumed he'd told you"). | County cup final: Toby, then Vera |

## Act 1 in detail — "Club nights"

Seven chapters, one per club regular, then the knockout cup. Each chapter is roughly one week. The story happens only in bubbles before and after games (and in the noticeboard on Home), never as cutscenes.

### Trial night — "New faces"

- **Setting:** the back room of the Red Lion. Folding tables, a tea urn, a mismatched knight in one of the sets.
- **Beats:**
  - Marjorie greets you and does the introductions, as she does for everyone (this is how the player learns who people are: nobody has a label).
  - Graham wants your name spelt correctly for the membership list.
  - Coach Pemberton watches from the side, arms folded, and says very little.
  - Halfway through the evening, Toby turns up. He's new too. Everyone likes him within ten minutes.
  - After game 5: Pemberton gives you your starting rating like it's a verdict. Then he spends longer talking to Toby.
- **Seeds:** the honours board on the wall. One name has been painted over and repainted: V. Hart **[?]**, "Club champion" for several years in a row. Marjorie: "Board two. Honestly. Thirty years."

### Chapter 1 — Marjorie · "First proper club night"

- Marjorie takes you under her wing: tea, gossip, and her London System.
- She's lovely. She also beats you in an endgame you thought was drawn, and apologises while doing it.
- **After the match:** "Well done. I'd have played on, but it's nearly ten."
- **Seed:** she mentions the club used to fill two rooms.

### Chapter 2 — Dex · "The streamer"

- A teenager with a phone propped against a water bottle. "It's not recording. Probably."
- He plays gambits and talks like the internet. He's annoying and, against your will, funny.
- **After you beat him:** "Don't clip that." (It is, of course, being clipped.)
- **Seed:** his viewer count goes up when you play him. He doesn't mention why.

### Chapter 3 — Oscar (and Neil) · "Junior night"

- Oscar is eleven, polite, silent and very quick. Neil, his dad, hovers.
- Neil has an excuse ready for every result and a boast for every other moment.
- **Beat:** Oscar is the only person who shakes your hand without looking at you or saying anything, and somehow it's the most respectful thing all week.

### Chapter 4 — Clive · "A quiet one"

- A retired engineer with a flask who offers a draw at move 12 every single time.
- **The lesson in story form:** Clive thinks wanting to win is slightly vulgar. Beating him means making a game out of nothing.
- **Seed:** Clive mentions he played board one "back when there was a proper board one." He changes the subject.

### Chapter 5 — Priya · "By the book"

- She's done every course and read every book. She knows twenty moves of theory, then stares at the board for twenty minutes.
- **Her small arc starts here:** after you beat her, she asks, quietly, "So what do you actually do when the book stops?"

### Chapter 6 — Graham · "Subs are due"

- The treasurer and rules stickler. Scoresheets, both copies.
- **The club's problem surfaces:** Graham has done a spreadsheet. The Red Lion wants more for the room. The numbers don't work.
- He doesn't make a drama of it. That's what makes it worrying.

### Chapter 7 — Toby · "No pressure, mate"

- You've seen him beat everyone you've beaten, slightly faster.
- He offers to "go over games together." He sends you his Lichess study. He's helpful. You can't justify disliking him.
- **Beat:** Coach Pemberton's scouting report on Toby is oddly thin, compared with the ones on everyone else. **[?]** (A first, tiny hint of the favouritism.)

### The knockout cup — "The club's oldest trophy"

- Three rounds against club regulars (Oscar, Clive, Graham), then the final.
- **Final:** Toby. Marjorie sets out the good set, the one without the mismatched knight.
- **If you lose:** "Good game! Want to go over it?" and Toby gets more supportive with every rematch, which is worse. Qualify again next month.
- **If you win:** Toby: "So deserved. Genuinely made up for you." Marjorie engraves the cup ("Well, the engraver needed it by Friday"). Pemberton congratulates you, then asks Toby to stay behind.
- **Act 1 closes on:** the noticeboard: a new sheet, "League team trials: the club ladder." Toby's name is already at the top **[?]**.

## Questions for you

1. **Vera's surname and the honours board.** Is "V. Hart" OK as a placeholder, and do you like the honours board as the way she's first introduced?
2. **How openly is Pemberton's favouritism hinted in Act 1?** My draft keeps it to two tiny moments (longer chats with Toby, a thin report on him). More, or less?
3. **The order of chapters.** Currently Marjorie, Dex, Oscar, Clive, Priya, Graham, Toby. Should Toby appear earlier than chapter 7, for example a friendly in chapter 3 or 4, to build the rivalry before the cup?
4. **Act 1's last beat.** Toby's name already at the top of the ladder: too much, or the right sting?
5. **Act 4's ending.** Toby taking a half-point bye to avoid you in the last round (from the tone guide) is a very funny, very petty ending. Is that the finale, or does the final round need a real game against him?
