# Chess App — Character Tone Guide

Sep 25, 2026 · @Joseph

## House style

Every line should sound like something a real person has actually said to you at work, school or a club. The humour is petty, understated and recognisable. If it sounds like a sitcom, cut it.

Nobody is a villain. Every character thinks they are being perfectly reasonable, and that is where the annoyance comes from.

### The three-question check

Every line must pass all three before it goes in:

1. Could a real person have said this to you at work, school or a club?
2. Does it tell you something without saying it outright?
3. Is it short enough to read between moves?

### Writing rules

- **Short.** About 15 words at most per bubble. Most lines are shorter.
- **Subtext, not statements.** Characters never explain how they feel or what is happening. The player works it out.
- **React to the board.** The best lines respond to the actual position, so the story and the chess feel like one thing.
- **Callbacks carry the story.** One line referring back to an earlier game tells more than a paragraph.
- **Never explain the joke.**

### The situations we draw on

Finding out last. Someone else taking credit. Being thanked for something small while your big thing goes unmentioned. Favouritism dressed up as fairness. "It's not personal." Being left out of the group chat. Helpful advice that is really a put-down. Grudges held for decades over nothing.

### Where lines appear

All dialogue appears beside the board: the character's portrait plus speech bubbles. There are no cutscenes or separate story screens.

| Moment | How much | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the game | 1 to 3 bubbles | Where most of the story lives |
| During the game | A few per game at most | Friendly games only, straight after a move, never while the player is thinking. None in league or tournament games (the real stage) |
| After the game | 1 or 2 bubbles | Then straight into the review |

Portraits need four or five expressions each: neutral, pleased, annoyed, surprised, smug.

## Getting the chess world right

Every line should survive being read by a regular club player or a serious online player. If someone in the know would wince, rewrite it.

### League and congress chess

- League matches are played over 4 to 6 boards, home and away. Board order roughly follows rating, with the captain allowed some leeway, which is exactly where grudges come from.
- Captains pick the team, fill in the match card, report the result and often write the match report for the club website.
- A team that can't field a player loses that board by default.
- English ratings are ECF ratings. Older players still say "grade", from the system used before 2020.
- Weekend tournaments (congresses) use Swiss pairings and sections by rating. Half-point byes and shared prizes are normal, and so is the resentment around them.

### Club-night habits

Touch-move. Pressing the clock with the same hand you moved with. Black starting White's clock. Phones off. Scoresheets. A handshake before and after. Going over the game together afterwards (the post-mortem). Casual "skittles" games and blitz once the league match is finished. Sets with a mismatched knight.

**Clocks:** friendlies and all of Act 1 are untimed, so clock lines (pressing with the wrong hand, Black starting White's clock) are tagged for timed matches only, from Act 2 onwards. A rule reminder like Marjorie's "Same hand for the clock, dear" is the one kind of remark allowed during a real match, as it would be at a real club.

### Online culture

"GG". Premoves. Flagging (winning or losing on time). "Mouse slip". The Bongcloud. "Google en passant". The evaluation bar and what Stockfish says. Bullet and blitz players looking down on slow games.

### Pitfalls to avoid

- **Nobody chats during a serious game.** Talking to your opponent is poor form and can break the rules. Chatter belongs in friendly club-night games (the assisted and guided stages). In league and tournament games (the real stage), characters speak only before and after, as real players do.
- **Nobody announces "Checkmate!"** dramatically in serious play.
- **Excuses are universal:** time trouble, a long week, "I was completely winning".
- **Ratings must fit the story.** A former county champion should be one of the strongest players in it.
- **Chess advice must be sound.** Toby's opening tips and the coach's scouting reports should be genuinely sensible.

## Toby, the rival

Toby joins the club the same night you do and is always slightly stronger. He is the final opponent of every act. He is warm, encouraging and helpful, and you cannot justify why you dislike him. He is genuinely very good and fake-humble about it: every win is luck, every result is "learning", and he goes straight to the top of the ladder without appearing to try.

**Voice:** Relentlessly positive. Calls you "mate". Softens everything with "honestly", "no pressure" and "no worries if not". Speaks in "we" when credit is going and "I" when he's doing well. Uses personal-growth language about ordinary decisions.

**He would never:** Gloat openly, swear, admit to being competitive, or apologise for anything that actually matters.

**Sample lines**

- *Game start:* "No pressure, mate. Honestly, just enjoy it."
- *You blunder:* "Ooh. No, that's fine. That's a learning moment."
- *Offering a draw when he's worse:* "Draw? Seems a fair result."
- *After beating you:* "Good game! Want to go over it?"
- *In the post-mortem:* "You were fine until the rook lift, honestly."
- *Recommending an opening:* "Have you looked at the Caro-Kann? Might suit you."
- *Sharing prep:* "I'll send you my Lichess study. No worries if not."
- *He loses:* "So deserved. Genuinely made up for you."
- *He blunders:* "Go on then. You've earned that one."
- *Steering into your weak spot:* "Endgame again? Sorry. I find them relaxing."
- *As captain, explaining the team:* "It's not all about ratings. It's about who's in form."
- *As captain, after your win saves the match:* "Really proud of how we handled it tonight." / "Match report's up. Gave you a mention."
- *Sitting down for the away match, after leaving your club:* "Hey! Didn't want this to be weird." / "It's not weird, is it?"
- *Beating you easily on trial night:* "Beginner's luck. Mine, I mean."
- *Losing to you in the last round of the final congress:* "Honestly, a draw was fine for me prize-wise. I wasn't really pushing." / "Long drive this morning. Not an excuse."

## Marjorie

Club secretary for 40 years. She runs the tea urn and knows everyone's business. She seems like a gentle early opponent, but she was a strong player decades ago and her endgames are quietly ruthless. She improves slowly throughout the story and never stops.

**Voice:** Polite, apologetic, faintly passive-aggressive. Delivers bad news as though you already knew it. Every compliment has a small hook in it.

**She would never:** Raise her voice, say anything directly unkind, or admit she's enjoying winning.

**Sample lines**

- *Game start:* "Phones off, love. Milk's on the side."
- *You press the clock with the wrong hand:* "Same hand for the clock, dear."
- *You pick up a piece and put it back:* "Touch-move, dear. I'm only saying."
- *Taking a piece:* "Oh dear. I'll have that, sorry love."
- *Converting an endgame:* "I'll just tidy these up."
- *You beat her:* "Well done. I'd have played on, but it's nearly ten."
- *On your rating:* "And what's that in old money? Grades, I mean."
- *Breaking the news of Toby leaving:* "Shame about Toby, isn't it." / "Oh — I assumed he'd told you."
- *On the Most Improved Player trophy:* "Well, the engraver needed it by Friday."
- *Returning after a losing streak:* "Lovely to see you. We kept your seat for a while."
- *Hinting at Vera's story:* "Board two. Honestly. Thirty years."

## Dex

A teenager who joined the club to make content for his stream. He starts as an irritating early opponent who attacks recklessly, and gradually becomes your teammate. He improves faster than anyone. He has secretly been streaming your games, and you are a minor meme.

**Voice:** Short, lower-case energy. Overconfident, deflects with jokes, allergic to sincerity. His loyalty only ever shows sideways.

**He would never:** Say anything openly nice to you, admit he's been studying, or explain internet slang to anyone.

**Sample lines**

- *Game start (early):* "It's not recording. Probably."
- *Opening with the Bongcloud:* "Respect the Bongcloud."
- *Sacrificing a piece:* "It's a sac. Trust me."
- *His attack fails:* "Mouse slip."
- *Capturing en passant:* "Google en passant."
- *In a slow game:* "This is so slow. I play bullet."
- *First league match:* "Wait, we can't premove?"
- *You beat him:* "Don't clip that."
- *Rematch at a higher level:* "Yeah, I've been studying. Don't make it weird."
- *Mid-season:* "Wait, you're not in the group chat?"
- *Revealing the stream:* "Your fork clip's on 40k views. You're fork guy now."
- *After Toby leaves:* "Never liked his flapjacks."

## Vera

A former county champion who left the club years ago after the coach put her on board two. She plays top board for the rival club, Kingsbridge. She is the shadow over the story, and whether she comes back is the final arc.

**Voice:** Few words, dry, exact. When she speaks, it lands. She never answers the question you asked.

**She would never:** Chat, explain herself, show she's rattled, or mention the falling-out directly.

**Sample lines**

- *First game against her:* "Board two, is it? That's nice."
- *Game start:* (She starts your clock without a word.)
- *You make a good move:* "Hm."
- *You blunder:* (nothing — she just plays the winning move)
- *You offer a draw:* "I'll play on."
- *You beat her:* "Well played. I've not been sleeping."
- *Asked to go over the game:* "I don't do post-mortems."
- *On Toby joining Kingsbridge:* "He'll do. Board three."
- *Late in the story:* "Is the urn still broken?"

## Coach Pemberton

A retired former club captain who presents the lessons and gives you a scouting report before each match on the opponent's actual style. He is the reason Vera left, and he has quietly been giving Toby your scouting reports too. His name is Pemberton. Nobody at a real English club gets called "Coach", so it's a nickname: Dex started it ironically and it stuck. Marjorie never uses it; she calls him by his first name, as she has for 40 years.

**Voice:** Confident, opinionated, a little old-fashioned. Gives genuinely useful chess advice, then undercuts it with favouritism or an old grudge. Justifies every unfair decision as sound judgement.

**He would never:** Admit a mistake, explain his team selections properly, or say Vera's name without a pause.

**Sample lines**

- *Scouting Dex:* "He'll sacrifice something before move 15. Take it and trade queens."
- *Scouting Vera:* "She'll want board one. She always did."
- *On engines:* "Put the engine away. What did you think?"
- *Post-mortems after the match:* "I'll look at yours after. Toby's had a very interesting Najdorf."
- *After a loss:* "You knew the plan. You just didn't fancy it."
- *On the old days:* "In my day we adjourned at ten and analysed all week."
- *When you find out about the scouting reports:* "I coach everyone. It's only fair."

## Graham

Club treasurer and unofficial rules authority. He chases membership fees, keeps immaculate scoresheets and knows the rulebook better than anyone. He gives the league season its stakes: the pub is putting up the room hire, and his spreadsheets say the club can't afford it without a good season.

**Voice:** Precise and procedural. Everything is "technically" or "strictly speaking". Not unkind, just unable to let anything go.

**He would never:** Bend a rule, round a number, or claim anything he isn't entitled to.

**Sample lines**

- *Game start:* "Scoresheet, please. Both copies."
- *You press the clock with the wrong hand:* "Same hand, please. I'll let it go this once."
- *Claiming a draw:* "That's the same position three times. I'm claiming."
- *After a club night:* "Subs are due, by the way. Not now. After."
- *You beat him:* "Fair result. It'll be on the website by Thursday."
- *On the room hire:* "They want another eight pounds a night." / "I've done a spreadsheet."
- *On Toby becoming captain:* "He did offer to do the fixtures. Nobody else did."

## Clive

A retired engineer who offers a draw around move 12 in every game and swaps pieces off whenever he can. Because draws are replayed in the app, he is a genuine lesson in creating winning chances against someone who doesn't want any.

**Voice:** Mild, reasonable and deflating. Makes wanting to win sound slightly unsporting.

**He would never:** Take a risk, sound excited, or admit he was lucky.

**Sample lines**

- *Friendly game start:* "Just a quiet one tonight, I think."
- *Move 12:* "Draw? I don't really see anything in it for either of us."
- *You decline:* "Fair enough. Your call."
- *Simplifying:* "Might as well swap those off."
- *You overpress and lose:* "I'd have taken the draw, if I were you."
- *You beat him:* "Well, you wanted it more."
- *On team selection:* "Board four suits me fine."

## Priya

She has read every opening book and done every online course. She knows 20 moves of theory in every line, then sinks into a 40-minute think the moment you leave it. Her small arc: she starts asking you what the plan is once the opening is over, which quietly reinforces that lesson for the player.

**Voice:** Earnest and quick. Talks in move numbers and references what the books say.

**She would never:** Admit she doesn't know a line, or play anything she hasn't studied.

**Sample lines**

- *Game start:* "I've been doing a course on this."
- *You leave the main line:* "That's not theory." / "Is that even good?"
- *Out of her preparation:* "I know this. I definitely know this."
- *Long think:* (She has been staring at the board for twenty minutes.)
- *She wins inside her preparation:* "That's all main line, to be fair."
- *You beat her:* "You weren't supposed to play that."
- *Later in the story:* "OK, so the book stops at move 19. Then what do you actually do?"

## Oscar and Neil

Oscar is the 11-year-old junior every club has. He plays quickly and confidently and is better every time you see him. He barely speaks. The pettiness comes from his dad, Neil, who hovers at the edge of the board and doesn't play.

**Oscar's voice:** Polite and minimal. Reacts the same way to winning and losing.

**Neil's voice:** Bragging dressed up as modesty. Has an excuse ready for every loss.

**Neither would ever:** Be openly rude. Oscar is never the joke; Neil is.

**Sample lines**

- *Oscar, game start:* (Shakes hands without looking up.)
- *Oscar wins:* "Good game." (Goes off to play blitz.)
- *Oscar loses:* "Good game." (Exactly the same.)
- *Neil, before your first game:* "He's only been playing since March."
- *Neil, after Oscar loses:* "He's had a long week at school."
- *Neil, later in the story:* "He's got a FIDE rating now."
- *Neil, generally:* "His coach says he's ahead of schedule."

## Rival club faces

### Felix, university captain

He has done deep opening preparation on his laptop and plays sharp lines at speed. He is excellent while he remembers his preparation and falls apart in quiet positions.

**Voice:** Casual and knowing. Treats deep theory as obvious.

**He would never:** Admit he's out of his preparation, or show he's rattled.

- *Game start:* "Sicilian again? Great."
- *Early on:* "I'm still in prep, by the way."
- *You play something unusual:* "Interesting. That's a sideline."
- *Out of his preparation:* (A long silence.)
- *You beat him:* "Didn't prep that."

### Derek, the veterans' club

Top board for a club full of retired players. He's an old-school grinder with a crushing handshake and a story about every tournament since 1983.

**Voice:** Hearty and anecdotal. Every result reminds him of an older one.

**He would never:** Resign early, or finish a story in fewer than three sentences.

- *Game start:* (The handshake goes on slightly too long.)
- *Before the game:* "I drew with a grandmaster once. Simul, but still."
- *Grinding an endgame:* "No rush. We've got all night."
- *You beat him:* "Good game. I'd blundered earlier, mind."
- *After the match:* "Reminds me of Hastings in '83."
